THE HISTORY BEHIND THE BARS
• Mozart began composing by age five and performed across Europe as a child prodigy, including before Empress Maria Theresa at Schönbrunn Palace in 1762 (where, by contemporary accounts, he played on a cloth-covered keyboard).
• In 1770, fourteen-year-old Mozart heard Gregorio Allegri's closely guarded Miserere at the Sistine Chapel in Rome and famously transcribed it from memory.
• In 1781 Mozart broke from the service of Salzburg's Prince-Archbishop Colloredo — literally kicked out the door by court steward Count Arco — to launch a freelance career in Vienna.
• The famous "too many notes, my dear Mozart" remark attributed to Emperor Joseph II comes from Franz Xaver Niemetschek's 1798 biography and remains debated by historians.
• Antonio Salieri served as Imperial Hofkapellmeister in Vienna for 36 years (1788–1824) and dominated European opera houses from Vienna to Paris.
• On February 7, 1786, Emperor Joseph II staged a head-to-head musical contest at Schönbrunn Palace Orangery (Mozart's Der Schauspieldirektor vs Salieri's Prima la musica e poi le parole); court opinion favored Salieri's Italian opera.
• Despite high earnings in the mid-1780s, Mozart struggled with debt and wrote repeatedly to fellow Freemason Michael Puchberg asking for loans; in early letters he also complained of "Salieri's cabals."
• Mozart catalogued over 600 works (Köchel catalogue up to K. 626) before dying on December 5, 1791, at age 35.
• Modern historians agree Salieri did NOT poison Mozart: the murder legend was popularized by Alexander Pushkin's 1830 drama Mozart and Salieri, Rimsky-Korsakov's opera, Peter Shaffer's play, and Miloš Forman's 1984 film Amadeus.
• Mozart's unfinished Requiem (K. 626) was anonymously commissioned via messenger by Count Franz von Walsegg, who intended to pass the work off as his own tribute to his late wife.
• In October 1791 Mozart himself wrote warmly to Constanze about taking Salieri to The Magic Flute, noting that Salieri listened with full attention and applauded every number with "Bravo!" and "Bello!"
• Salieri taught Ludwig van Beethoven, Franz Schubert, Franz Liszt, Johann Nepomuk Hummel — and after Mozart's death, Constanze Mozart entrusted her youngest son, Franz Xaver Wolfgang Mozart, to Salieri for composition lessons.
