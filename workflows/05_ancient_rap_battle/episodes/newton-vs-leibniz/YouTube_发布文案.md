• During the 1665–1666 plague closure of Cambridge, Isaac Newton developed his method of fluxions, optics and gravitation (the falling apple story comes from his later accounts to Stukeley and Conduitt).
• Newton published Principia Mathematica in 1687, while keeping his 1666 and 1671 fluxion tracts largely unpublished for decades.
• Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz earned his doctorate in law before turning to mathematics; he visited London in 1673 and 1676 and corresponded with Newton via Royal Society secretary Henry Oldenburg.
• Leibniz published the first calculus paper in 1684 (Nova Methodus) and introduced the dy/dx and integral ∫ notation still used worldwide today.
• Leibniz built the Stepped Reckoner mechanical calculator (whose multi-digit carry mechanism remained finicky with period craftsmanship) and published his binary arithmetic paper in 1703.
• Newton spent decades on private alchemical experiments alongside his work in physics and mathematics.

• Newton became Warden (1696) and Master of the Mint (1699) and President of the Royal Society (1703).
• In 1712 the Royal Society's Commercium Epistolicum committee ruled in Newton's favor; Newton himself secretly drafted the report and its anonymous review.
• In 1714 Leibniz's patron Georg Ludwig of Hanover became King George I of Great Britain, leaving Leibniz behind in Hanover to write the Guelph family history until his death in 1716.
• Newton lost heavily (often estimated around £20,000) in the 1720 South Sea Bubble crash; the famous quote "I cannot calculate the madness of the people" is attributed to him in 18th-century sources.
